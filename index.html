● SemiAnalysis · Feature

To Boldly Go: the real case for space data centers

SemiAnalysis pressure-tests the orbital-compute hype. Using today's tech, a space deployment runs ~$8.64/GPU-hr vs ~$2.37 on the ground — a >4x gap in 2026 driven by launch costs and a 5-year orbital lifespan. Their model sees parity only ~2040 as launch falls toward ~$250/kg; space becomes a necessity only if terrestrial power and permitting starve supply first (the "Elon Musk scenario").

Floating point: the origin story

The Chip Letter traces the notation now baked into "floating point units" across modern ML silicon back to the earliest computing — mantissa, exponent and base as a compact way to span the scale of the universe, motivated by astronomy and navigation tables since Babbage.

📊 Business & Strategy

The Business Engineer · Prof G

Two structural arguments framed the week: how to build product against an exponential model curve, and who pays when automation finally reaches the road.

The Product Overhang Doctrine

The Business Engineer's thesis: model capability compounds (per METR, autonomous task horizon expanded ~41x in 16 months) while product capability moves linearly. The widening gap — "overhang" — is the key strategic surface; winners ship into capability that doesn't exist yet, betting the next model release arrives to meet it.

Why it matters

Once an exponential and a linear curve share an axis, the gap only widens. Roadmaps built for "the capability you can see today" are structurally mispriced — the bet is a pipeline of staggered, decaying arbitrage.
